Home · motion What the waiting screen should claim A partner opens Home many times a day and most of those times it is empty. Motion is the only thing that separates we are working on it from you have been forgotten — so each candidate below is judged on what it honestly claims, not on how it looks.
Scanning
A · The Scanner Claims: we are covering ground near you, right now. Risk: a bare left-to-right bar reads as a progress bar. Sweeping over a scene with a pause between passes is what stops it promising completion.
Nothing booked yet today
B · The Daybook Claims: here is your day, and it is still open. Risk: calm to the point of flat. It says nothing about dispatch working — the anxiety it soothes is a different one.
C · The Switch Claims: you are on, and being listened to. Risk: the switch takes the whole first screen, so today's money drops below the fold — on the app's most-checked figure.

The rule this enforces An animation is a claim about what the system is doing. A sweeping beam over "you are offline" would be the app performing work nobody asked it to do — the visual equivalent of a spinner on a request that was never sent. Offline is still, and the one moving thing on it is the control that fixes it.
The motion budget — everything that moves, and what it represents
The beam / wave Dispatch is running for this Partner. Stops dead when they go offline.
Live dot halo A heartbeat landed. It is the one thing on screen proving the session is alive.
Numbers rising on open A real figure arriving from the server — once, not looping. A number that keeps animating is a number nobody can read.
Cards entering, staggered Work arrived while they were looking. The stagger is what makes two new cards read as two, not one block.
Going on / off The biggest state change in the app. Worth the most motion of anything here — and the only place a press should feel physical.
Never animated Anything standing in for data we do not have. No "3 jobs near you" ticker, no invented nearby count, no fake queue position.
Every loop above is pure CSS on a transform or an opacity — no animation library, no rAF loop, nothing that keeps a mid-range Android's CPU awake. All of it collapses to a still frame under prefers-reduced-motion.
